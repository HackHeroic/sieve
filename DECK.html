SIEVE

Finding real patterns in time series, when the usual way of checking finds thousands of fake ones

HackMIT 2026 · Voloridge "Signal in the Noise"

1 / 12

The problem

Mine a dataset for patterns and you will find thousands.
Most are artifacts of having looked.

The standard defence is multiple-testing correction. Every standard correction assumes your observations are independent.

Hourly data is not. On our outcome the lag-1 autocorrelation is 0.654.

So the analytic p-values are invalid, and the usual fix — shuffle the labels and see what chance produces — is invalid too, because shuffling destroys the very structure that makes chance alignments possible.

2 / 12

How badly does that matter? We measured it.

120 datasets built to contain no signal at all, by circularly shifting the real outcome so the autocorrelation stays real but the relationship is destroyed.

methodfalse positive rate
naive row shuffle1.000
circular block permutation (ours)0.058

The standard approach declares a discovery on every single one of 120 datasets that contain nothing.

Ours fires on 7, against a nominal 5%. Exact binomial test of correct calibration: p = 0.672.

3 / 12

Why the naive null fails

White noise cannot accidentally line up with an autocorrelated rule for long stretches, so the naive null never reaches extreme values, so its bar is four times too lenient and 18,000 extra rules walk under it.

4 / 12

What SIEVE does

  1. Stratify the outcome before searching. Top decile within each hour-of-day by calendar-month cell, so seasonality cannot masquerade as a discovery.
  2. Every predicate knowable ahead of time. Forecast-derived, lagged ≥24h, or calendar. Otherwise you explain the past.
  3. Fisher ranks; it does not decide. We get p = 1.4e-166 and discard it as a probability. It orders candidates. Inference comes from permutation.
  4. Westfall-Young min-P over all 66,844 rules: the distribution of the best-looking rule findable in noise.
  5. Circular block permutation, stratified by month. 168-hour blocks. This is what makes the null valid.

Plus Tarone's testability bound, which contributes 0.36% here. We report that as a null result rather than dropping it quietly.

Does it work on anything else?

Five grids are five slices of one dataset. So we pointed it at NOAA GHCN-Daily — different agency, different domain, daily not hourly, no shared code.

The correction scales with the dependence it exists to correct for, and vanishes when that dependence is absent.

It does not always disagree with the naive method. It disagrees exactly when, and in proportion to how much, the naive assumption is violated. The tool also tells you when you do not need it.

Speed, and the number we refused

122× end to end. Not 3,134× against naive Python.

The pod's own throttled CPU would have supported a 16,000× claim. It is in our repository as the number we threw away.

Our CPU baseline is single-threaded, unfused numpy at 6.8 GB/s on a machine that sustains 100-400. Against a tuned implementation the win is low double digits. The GPU is not the contribution — PBOOST (2014) and HWY (2015) did GPU permutation testing a decade ago.

11 / 12

What is wrong with it

An independent five-dimension audit confirmed 24 defects. The load-bearing ones, disclosed rather than discovered by someone else:

  • The data window. EIA renamed fuel columns in mid-2024; an unguarded notna().all() silently drops every row after 2024-07-02. 31.5% of the data.
  • A blind spot. hour_sin and hour_cos are constant within each binarisation cell, so 8 of 85 predicates never fire and no time-of-day rule is expressible.
  • Forecast vintage. EIA files one D-1 vintage for all 24 hours, so lag-24 features are partly look-ahead relative to the filing instant. Tested: restricting to lag ≥48h drops 94% of the lattice and lift falls 4.61 → 3.52. The effect survives.
  • Assumed, not proven. Subset pivotality for strong FWER. Block permutation is asymptotically valid, not exact — which is why we measured the false positive rate instead of claiming a theorem.
12 / 12

What this solves

Anyone mining patterns from a dependent time series with conventional multiple testing will ship false discoveries.

On data containing nothing, the conventional approach reported a discovery 120 times out of 120.

That is the whole case. Not "here is a better number", but "the thing you would otherwise have done fails completely, and here is the measurement."

What we do not claim. We have not shown day-ahead forecast errors are expensive enough to make this a painkiller for a grid operator specifically. No cost figure was measured and none is claimed. The grid is the demonstration domain; the method is the contribution. We know when forecasts fail, not why.
