The problem, measured

We built datasets containing no signal at all — real autocorrelation preserved, every relationship destroyed — and ran both methods on them.

The standard approach reports a discovery on 98.8% of market datasets that contain nothing. Nominal is 5%.

Rows in a time series are not exchangeable. Shuffling them destroys the structure that lets chance produce long false alignments, so the bar you set from the shuffle is far too low. This is the data-snooping problem with the dependence left in.

It rediscovered volatility clustering

yesterday's |ΔVIX| was extreme AND the day before was too → today's |ΔVIX| is extreme

That is Mandelbrot's 1963 definition verbatim: large changes are followed by large changes, of either sign. We verified it rather than asserting it:

testresult
ACF of signed ΔVIX (lags 1,2,5)−0.125, −0.035, 0.008
ACF of absolute |ΔVIX|0.366, 0.364, 0.283
Ljung–Box Q(10) on |ΔVIX|7,446.7   p = 0
Engle ARCH LM test1,817.8 on 10 df   p = 0

Engle's 1982 ARCH test is the one the 2003 Nobel was awarded for. No finance was supplied to the miner.

And it is not merely restating persistence: conditioning on the previous two days alone gives 4.09×; SIEVE's conjunction gives 5.57×.

5 / 12

This is a backtesting problem

Quant finance already solved the correction half. White's Reality Check (2000), Hansen's SPA (2005), Romano–Wolf StepM (2005), Deflated Sharpe, PBO. All resampling-based. All handle dependence through a block or stationary bootstrap.

Every one of them needs a block length. Romano–Wolf has a section titled "Choice of Block Sizes". Nobody publishes how they chose theirs.

block covers the dependenceFWER 0.062, as advertised
block too short by 4×FWER 0.362
block too short by 24×FWER 0.863

If your backtest's block bootstrap uses a block shorter than your strategy's dependence horizon, your data-snooping correction is not doing what the paper says — and nothing errors, so you never find out. The other half, searching a hypothesis lattice rather than enumerating a fixed grid, is where finance stops: Sullivan–Timmermann–White's 7,846 trading rules are a Cartesian product of five templates, fixed before seeing data.

6 / 12

Why the conventional null fails

White noise cannot accidentally track an autocorrelated rule for a long stretch, so the naive null never reaches extreme values and its cutoff sits far too close to zero. Everything downstream inherits that error.

7 / 12

How it works

  1. Stratify the outcome first — worst decile within each calendar cell, so seasonality cannot masquerade as a discovery.
  2. Every predicate knowable in advance — lagged, derived, or calendar.
  3. Fisher ranks, it does not decide. We compute p = 1e−166 and discard it as a probability: rows are not independent. It orders candidates.
  4. Westfall–Young min–P over the whole lattice — the distribution of the best-looking rule findable in noise.
  5. Circular block permutation, at a length chosen by measuring the false positive rate.

The correction scales with the dependence it exists to correct for, and vanishes when that dependence is absent.

On daily weather the outcome is nearly white noise, so the naive shuffle was fine all along — and SIEVE says so. It does not always disagree. It disagrees exactly when, and in proportion to how much, the assumption is violated. A dose–response relationship is stronger evidence for a mechanism than any single number. Four datasets, three agencies, three domains, one tool.

The effect travels to every grid tested. Formal significance travels in 25 of 57, concentrated in CISO where 182-day blocks leave only 10 exchangeable units and the test is very low-powered.

The holdout fails. Lift persists out of sample at 2.86 and 512 test-set rules are significant, but the specific top-20 rules do not survive correction over 60,576 hypotheses on 18 months.

10 / 12

We found a defect in the federal data

PJM's EIA-930 day-ahead forecast leads its own realised demand by exactly one hour, from 2019-01-30 onward.

A lower error under a shift proves nothing alone — it is equally consistent with "demand is late", which needs the opposite fix. Two structural tests:

  • Geography. PJM's demand leads Eastern peers at lag 0 and Central peers at −1 — real geography. PJM's forecast leads every peer at −1 regardless of timezone. Timezone invariance means a label offset.
  • Conservation. Demand = NetGen − Interchange holds at lag 0, median residual 7 MW, 17.4% exact zeros. So the forecast is what moved.

PJM alone of 55 balancing authorities, all 8 years, start date pinned. Four rows also read 2,147,480,000 MW = 231, an overflow sentinel.

Correcting it cut our headline lift 4.61 → 2.97 and raised the conventional method's inflation 1.78× → 7.86×. Fixing our own defect made the case four times stronger.

What is wrong, and what this is for

  • Data window. EIA renamed fuel columns mid-2024; an unguarded notna().all() silently drops 31.5% of the rows.
  • Blind spot. Two time-of-day features are constant inside each binarisation cell, so 8 of 85 predicates never fire and no time-of-day rule is expressible.
  • The holdout fails, and the earlier 20/20 and 57/58 are withdrawn — computed on the uncorrected series and on grids where error was not controlled.
  • Asymptotic, not exact — which is why calibration is measured, never claimed.
  • The GPU is not the contribution. PBOOST (2014) and HWY (2015) did GPU permutation testing a decade ago.

Mine a dependent time series with conventional multiple testing and you will ship false discoveries. On market data containing nothing, the conventional approach reported a discovery 98.8% of the time. Ours reported 6.2% — and only once we measured the one parameter nobody publishes.
