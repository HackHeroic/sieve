SIEVE

Mining patterns from dependent time series, where the standard way of checking your work reports a discovery on every dataset that contains nothing

HackMIT 2026 · Voloridge "Signal in the Noise"

1 / 10

The problem, in one experiment

We built 120 datasets containing no signal at all — real autocorrelation preserved, every relationship destroyed — and ran both methods on all 120.

methoddatasets it called a discovery
standard multiple-testing correction120 of 120
SIEVE8 of 120

The conventional approach fires on every single one. Ours fires at the nominal 5% rate: 0.067, exact binomial p = 0.40.

Rows in a time series are not exchangeable. Shuffling them destroys the very structure that lets chance produce long false alignments, so the bar you set from the shuffle is far too low.

2 / 10

Why the standard null fails

White noise cannot accidentally track an autocorrelated rule for a long stretch, so the naive null never reaches extreme values, so its cutoff sits four times too close to zero. On PJM it lets 30,883 rules through where ours passes 3,928 — 7.86x inflation.

3 / 10

The result we did not expect to find

Block length is the one parameter the method needs. We assumed one value worked everywhere. It does not, and getting it wrong is catastrophic:

gridoutcome ACF at 168hFPR at 504h blockscalibrated blockFPR there
PJM0.0070.067504h (21d)0.067
MISO0.0840.083504h0.083
CISO0.2110.8504368h (182d)0.033

Same code, same procedure, one parameter. Error rate 0.067 or 0.850 depending on whether the block covers the dependence.

CISO's autocorrelation is still 0.152 at three weeks where PJM's is 0.001. Give it six-month blocks and it calibrates. Block length is a measurement, not a convention — and this is the same failure mode as choosing a bootstrap block length badly in a backtest.

4 / 10

It knows when it is not needed

We pointed the same tool at NOAA daily weather — different agency, different domain, daily not hourly, no shared feature code.

outcome ACF, lag 1naive / SIEVE inflation
PJM, hourly0.7197.86×
NOAA, daily0.0281.03×

The correction scales with the dependence it exists to correct for, and vanishes when that dependence is absent.

On daily weather the outcome is nearly white noise, so the naive shuffle was fine all along — and SIEVE says so. It does not always disagree. It disagrees exactly when, and in proportion to how much, the assumption is violated. A dose-response relationship is stronger evidence for a mechanism than any single number.

5 / 10

How it works

  1. Stratify the outcome first. Worst decile within each hour-of-day by calendar-month cell, so seasonality cannot masquerade as a discovery.
  2. Every predicate knowable 24h ahead. Forecast-derived, lagged, or calendar.
  3. Fisher ranks, it does not decide. We compute p = 1e-166 and discard it as a probability — rows are not independent. It orders candidates.
  4. Westfall-Young min-P over all 66,817 rules: the distribution of the best-looking rule findable in noise.
  5. Circular block permutation, stratified by month, at a block length chosen by measuring the false positive rate.

Plus Tarone's testability bound, which contributes 0.36% here — reported as a measured null result rather than dropped quietly.

The effect travels to every grid we tested. Formal significance travels in 25 of 57, and the gap is concentrated in CISO, where six-month blocks leave only 10 exchangeable units and the test is very low-powered.

The holdout fails. Lift persists out of sample at 2.86 and 512 test-set rules are significant, but the specific top-20 rules do not survive correction over 60,576 hypotheses on 18 months.

7 / 10

We found a defect in the federal data

PJM's EIA-930 day-ahead forecast leads its own realised demand by exactly one hour, from 2019-01-30 onward.

A lower error under a shift proves nothing on its own — it is equally consistent with "demand is late", which needs the opposite fix. Two structural tests:

  • Geography. PJM's demand leads Eastern peers at lag 0 and Central peers at lag −1 — real geography. PJM's forecast leads every peer at −1 regardless of timezone. Timezone invariance means a label offset.
  • Conservation. Demand = NetGen − Interchange holds for PJM at lag 0, median residual 7 MW, 17.4% exact zeros. So the forecast is what moved.

PJM alone of 55 balancing authorities. All 8 years. Start date pinned. Four rows also carry 2,147,480,000 MW = 231, an overflow sentinel.

Correcting it cut our headline lift from 4.61 to 2.97 — and raised the naive method's inflation from 1.78× to 7.86×. Fixing our own defect made the case four times stronger.

9 / 10

What is wrong with it

  • The data window. EIA renamed fuel columns mid-2024 and an unguarded notna().all() silently drops every row after 2024-07-02 — 31.5% of the data.
  • A blind spot. Two time-of-day features are constant inside each binarisation cell, so 8 of 85 predicates never fire and no time-of-day rule is expressible.
  • Forecast vintage. One D-1 vintage covers all 24 hours, so lag-24 features are partly look-ahead relative to filing. Tested: restricting to lag≥48h drops 94% of the lattice and the effect survives.
  • The holdout fails, and the old 20/20 and 57/58 are withdrawn — they were computed on the uncorrected series and on grids where error was not controlled.
  • Asymptotic, not exact. Which is why calibration is measured, not claimed.
10 / 10

What this is for

Mine a dependent time series with conventional multiple testing and you will ship false discoveries.

On 120 datasets containing nothing, the conventional approach reported a discovery 120 times. Ours reported 8. Set the block length wrong and ours reports 85 — so we measure it instead of assuming it.

That is the contribution. Not "here is a better number", but "the thing you would otherwise have done fails completely, here is the measurement, and here is the one parameter that decides whether the fix works."

What we do not claim. We have not shown day-ahead forecast errors cost a grid operator enough to make this a painkiller for them specifically. No cost figure was measured. The grid is the demonstration; the method is the contribution. And every single-factor driver we found is already documented in the power-systems literature — the interactions are not.
